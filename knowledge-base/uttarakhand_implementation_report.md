## 3 Section 11 Deposit and Refund Provisions

### 3.1 Section 11(1) Security Deposit Cap

- Provision: The security deposit to be paid by the tenant in advance shall be such as may be agreed upon between the landlord and the tenant in the tenancy agreement, which shall (a) not exceed two months rent, in case of residential premises; and (b) Not exceed six months rent, in case of non-residential premises.

- Statutory Cap: 2 months rent (residential), 6 months rent (non-residential)

- Key Qualification: Deposit must be agreed upon between landlord and tenant, but cannot exceed the statutory cap

- Deposit Above Cap: Any amount above the statutory cap must be refunded regardless of any other deductions

- Primary Source: Uttarakhand Gazette (SRC-02)

- Verification Status: Verified - cap of 2 months (residential) / 6 months (non-residential)

### 3.2 Section 11(2) Refund of Security Deposit

- Provision: The security deposit shall be refunded to the tenant on the date of taking over vacant possession of the premises from the tenant, after making due deduction of any liability of the tenant.

- Refund Trigger: Date of taking over vacant possession of premises

- Allowable Deductions: Any liability of the tenant (unpaid rent, damages beyond normal wear and tear, etc.)

- Deposit Above Cap: Any deposit paid above the statutory cap in S 11(1) must be refunded - the cap is mandatory

- Primary Source: Uttarakhand Gazette (SRC-02)

- Verification Status: Verified - refund on vacant possession after liability deduction


## 4 Section 47 Repeal and Savings

### 4.1 Repeal

- Provision: The State Rent Control Act, which is in force immediately before the commencement of this Act, is hereby repealed.

- Legal Effect: The State Rent Control Act is repealed as of the commencement date (01.01.2023)

- Scope: Only the State Rent Control Act is repealed; this Act continues in force

### 4.2 Savings Provision

- Provision: Notwithstanding such repeal, all cases and other proceedings under the said State Rent Control Act pending at the commencement of this Act shall be continued and disposed of in accordance with the provisions of the State Rent Control Act, as if that Act had continued to be in force and this Act had not been enacted.

- Legal Effect: Pending cases under the old State Rent Control Act continue under the old Act's framework

- Practical Implication: The repeal is legal, not administrative - procedural continuity for pending cases

- Operational Question: Whether the actual case management systems have transitioned from the old Act to the new Act requires verification

- Primary Source: Uttarakhand Gazette (SRC-02)

- Verification Status: Verified - repeal and savings provision text confirmed; operational continuity unverified

### 4.3 Key Distinction: Legal Repeal vs. Administrative Continuity

- Repeal is legal, not administrative. Pending Rent Control Act cases continue under old act framework.

- Actual operational continuity must be verified - this is not automatically guaranteed by the statutory repeal.

## 6 Authority Appointment and Operational Status

- Provision: Section 30 - District Collector or District Magistrate may appoint an officer not below the rank of Deputy Collector as Rent Authority

- Appointment Authority: District Collector or District Magistrate

- Minimum Rank: Officer not below the rank of Deputy Collector

- Status: Appointment power identified in the Act; actual appointments require verification per district
